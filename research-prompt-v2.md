## Critical instruction on register

Do **not** write in academic, analytical, or report prose. No executive summary. No "this analysis examines." No hedged institutional phrasing. Write in plain, direct, spoken English — short sentences, concrete nouns. Where a section asks for a list, give me a bare list and nothing else: no introductory paragraph, no synthesis, no concluding thoughts.

If you find yourself writing a sentence longer than 20 words, break it.

## Critical instruction on staying on-topic

Every fact you give me exists to serve **[TOPIC]**, not whatever it happens to lead to. Downstream causes and effects are welcome — but before including one, check: *would a first-time viewer still recognize this as being about [TOPIC]?* If a chain of causes has wandered into a different subject wearing this one's name (a story about a product collapse turning into a story about port logistics, or a story about a company turning into a lecture on an unrelated regulation), stop the chain earlier or leave it out. Depth is good. Drift is not depth.

## PART 1 — The commonly told version

In 5-6 plain sentences: the version of this story that most articles and videos already tell. I need this only so I know what to avoid repeating. Do not embellish it.

## PART 2 — The gap

What do primary sources, court records, filings, regulatory reports, contemporaneous press, or non-English coverage say that the common version leaves out, softens, or gets wrong?

For each item: the fact, the source, and one line on why popular coverage missed it.

Prioritise: who could have stopped this and didn't; what contradicts the standard account; the most specific overlooked detail; what happened *after* the main event that coverage never followed up on.

## PART 3 — Verified facts

Every fact I might state on camera, each with its source. Mark clearly:
- **[PRIMARY]** — you read the filing, transcript, court document or report itself
- **[SECONDARY]** — a news article or summary describing one of the above
- **[UNCERTAIN]** — sources disagree, or you're inferring

Flag anything resting on a single source, especially claims about a living person.

**If any figure moves or compounds over time** (a debt total that grows, a valuation that falls, a body count that rises, a fine that escalates) — give the figure at every stage you have a source for, and check that consecutive stages are actually consistent with the rate or cause driving the change. If they aren't, say so plainly rather than presenting both.

## PART 4 — Texture (this is the most important section — do not compress it)

I am filming this. I need things a camera can photograph and a narrator can point at. **No analysis in this section. Raw lists only.**

1. **Physical objects** that genuinely appear in this story — what people held, wore, sat in, signed, drove, stacked in a warehouse, ate. Be specific. "A briefcase" is weak; "a Denny's coffee cup" is what I want.
2. **Named places**, each with one distinguishing physical feature.
3. **Recognisable prices** — what a normal person would recognise: a ticket price, a monthly rent, a salary, a sticker price.
4. **Direct quotes** where someone said something surprising, irritated, arrogant, or human. Exact wording and source. Not corporate statements.
5. **Absurd or ironic true details** — the small things that sound invented but aren't. The detail a viewer would repeat to a friend the next day.
6. **Sensory friction** — queues, smells, waiting, broken things, weather, the physical experience of being there.
7. **Someone doing something** — a named or describable person performing a specific action at a specific moment. Not "the company decided." A person, doing a thing.

Aim for 10+ items minimum across these. If you can only find three, say so plainly rather than padding with generalities.

## PART 5 — Showable assets

Every specific artifact that could appear on screen as-is: a tweet, a screenshot, a photograph, a named document page, a headline, a chart, a piece of footage. For each: what it shows, where it is, why it's compelling.

## PART 6 — The pivotal detail

One fact, document, or quote. The one that most changes how this story should be told. One paragraph on why. If it were cut, what would be lost?

## PART 7 — Open questions

What's genuinely unresolved or contradictory across sources. Don't tidy this up.
