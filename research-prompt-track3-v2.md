## Critical instruction on register

Do **not** write in academic, analytical, or report prose. No executive summary. No "this analysis examines." No hedged institutional phrasing. Write in plain, direct, spoken English — short sentences, concrete nouns. Where a section asks for a list, give me a bare list.

If you find yourself writing a sentence longer than 20 words, break it.

## Critical instruction on sourcing depth — different from Track 1, on purpose

This is not a primary-source excavation. There's no filing to dig up and no cover-up to uncover — the mechanism is publicly understood, the job is explaining it well. **What matters is one authoritative figure per claim, not a citation trail.** A reputable textbook, a central bank explainer, an established encyclopedia entry, or a well-regarded educational source is a fine anchor here — it would be a weak one on a Track 1 exposé. Don't force primary-document sourcing onto a mechanism that doesn't have primary documents; that produces the over-cited, academic register this whole prompt exists to avoid. Depth here means texture and worked examples, not footnotes.

## PART 1 — What people get wrong, or don't know at all

In 4-5 plain sentences: either the specific wrong intuition most people have about this mechanism, or, if there's no wrong belief, the thing about it almost nobody actually knows despite using it daily. This is the prior the whole episode exists to correct or reveal — be precise about what it actually is, not just "people don't understand X."

## PART 2 — The mechanism, broken into stages

Break the mechanism into 3-5 discrete engines or steps — the smallest number that actually explains it, not the most exhaustive. For each:
- **One physical model or everyday analogy** it can be shown as (a tug-of-war, a scale tipping, water finding its level, two islands trading)
- **One authoritative figure** that makes the step concrete — a rate, a ratio, a real threshold
- **What happens if you remove or reverse this one step** — this is usually the fastest way to show why it matters

Order them so each step needs the one before it. Don't announce all the steps up front — I'll place that in the script; just give me the steps themselves, clearly separated.

## PART 3 — Real anchor figures

Every number I might state on camera, each with a source. Use the same marks as always:
- **[PRIMARY]** — an official source, a central authority, the actual data-publishing body
- **[SECONDARY]** — a reputable explainer, textbook, or established educational source describing it
- **[UNCERTAIN]** — sources disagree, or figures are estimates/ranges by nature

A range is an acceptable answer where the real thing genuinely varies (say so, and give the range). A single invented-sounding precise figure for something inherently variable is not.

## PART 4 — Texture (do not compress this — it matters more here than on any other track)

Explainers fail by turning into lectures. This section is the fix. **No analysis. Raw lists only.**

1. **Physical objects** connected to the mechanism — what someone would actually hold, look at, or point to. Be specific.
2. **A concrete everyday moment** where an ordinary person runs into this mechanism without realizing it.
3. **Recognisable numbers** — prices, rates, or amounts a normal viewer already has an intuition for, to anchor the abstract figures against.
4. **A real historical episode** where this mechanism visibly broke, spiked, or produced a strange result — a natural extreme case, not invented.
5. **The counter-intuitive fact** — the one thing that surprises people who think they already understand this.
6. **A joke-shaped detail** — something genuinely a little absurd about how this actually works, that isn't invented, just under-told.

Aim for 10+ items. If you can only find a few, say so rather than padding.

## PART 5 — The escalation case

Push the mechanism to an edge case or extreme, worked through step by step with real or realistic figures — hyperinflation for a currency mechanism, a monopoly for a market mechanism, a cascading failure for a systems mechanism. This is usually where the episode's most memorable beat lives, and where an A10-style escalation ladder or an A7-style closing binary gets its material.

## PART 6 — Where this gets boring

Name the specific point where an explanation like this usually loses a general audience — the paragraph that turns into a definition, the moment jargon becomes necessary, the step that's hardest to make physical. Say what's genuinely hard to dramatize here, don't just assure me it's all fine.
