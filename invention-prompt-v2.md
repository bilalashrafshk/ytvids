# FinanceCraft — Invention Prompt (v2, Track 2 / "The Hypothetical")

*The counterpart to the Research Prompt. Use this for POV scenarios, thought experiments, and any invented-scenario episode. Replace `[PREMISE]` and `[MECHANIC]`. Do not use the Track 1 research prompt for these.*

---

I'm building a fictional scenario episode for a financial documentary channel. Rough premise: **[PREMISE]**. The real financial or physical mechanic I want it to teach: **[MECHANIC]**.

This is invented, not reported. Do not search for real events to cite. Do not give me studies, statistics, or academic sources. The only thing that must be factually correct is the mechanic itself.

## Register

Plain spoken English. Short sentences. Concrete nouns. No analysis, no framing paragraphs, no summary. Where I ask for a list, give a bare list.

---

## PART 1 — Five premises, not one

Give me five versions of this scenario, and make at least two of them uncomfortable — past the point of reasonable. For each, one line only.

Use these angles:
1. **The constraint twist** — add a rule that makes the obvious path impossible
2. **The wrong protagonist** — the position nobody picks to tell it from
3. **The inversion** — the recovery instead of the collapse; the person who called it early and got punished
4. **The absurd literalisation** — take the abstraction and give it physical weight and a location
5. **The compressed clock** — force a decade into a week, or stretch an instant across a year

Then: which is strongest, and one sentence on why each of the other four is weaker.

## PART 2 — The contract

The scenario's rules, stated the way a legal notice would state them:
- What just happened (one sentence)
- The clock — how long
- The penalty — what is lost when it runs out
- The rules — what is and isn't allowed inside the scenario
- **The adversary** — the thing working against the protagonist *while they act*. Compounding interest, spreading failure, a closing window. It must be expressible as a number that moves.

All five must fit in 80 spoken words. Write them that way.

## PART 3 — The world kit

Invented, but specific. Vague is the failure mode here, not fabrication.

1. **Three named places**, each with one distinguishing physical feature
2. **Three recurring objects** the viewer sees more than once and learns to read
3. **One recurring human figure** who says almost nothing and is never explained
4. **One absurd-feeling detail per act** — the kind a viewer repeats to a friend

## PART 4 — Third-order consequences

For the four biggest beats, push the chain three steps past the obvious:

> first order (what everyone predicts) → second order → **third order (the beat worth filming)**

Example shape: *the feed dies → logins break → a warehouse can't dispatch because its logistics portal authenticates through the same provider → a pallet of frozen goods sits on a Rotterdam loading dock and the spoilage claim is denied because the contract requires digital proof of handover.*

Stop at first order and I have a list of things the viewer already assumed. I want scenes.

## PART 5 — The mechanic, verified (the only research in this prompt)

Confirm the mechanic works the way I've described. Specifically:
- Does the math actually behave this way? Show it.
- What realistic figures anchor it — real rates, real thresholds, real physical limits, real timelines?
- Where does my premise get the mechanic **wrong**? Say so directly.

If the mechanic is broken, the scenario is worthless no matter how good the story is. Be blunt here.

## PART 6 — The quotable beat

One moment. The single thing a viewer describes to someone else the next day. If you can't identify one, say so — that means the premise isn't ready.

## PART 7 — Where this gets boring

Be honest: which parts of this scenario are predictable, which beats have been done, and where would a viewer stop watching?

---

## Output rules

- Bare lists where lists are asked for
- Invented people, companies, places and dialogue are expected and wanted — be specific, not careful
- Real companies, products and institutions may be named as things the scenario *acts upon* (buying them, losing access to them). Never invent quotes, motives, or wrongdoing for a real entity.
- No studies, no citations, no statistics — except in Part 5
- "Not sure" is an acceptable answer; invented certainty is not
