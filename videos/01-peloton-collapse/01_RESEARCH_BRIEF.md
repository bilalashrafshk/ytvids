## 2. The Gap List (Departure from Conventional Coverage)
*Specific facts, timeline discrepancies, and forensic details absent or misconstrued in mainstream accounts:*

1. **The "Mr. Big / Ryan Reynolds" Distraction vs. Industrial Overreach**: Pop culture media blamed Peloton’s downfall on the *Sex and the City* reboot heart-attack episode and the holiday ad parody. In reality, the mortal blow had already been struck months earlier when executive leadership committed $420M in cash to acquire Precor and broke ground on a $400M factory in Ohio right before consumer mobility normalized.
2. **The Inverted CAC Math**: Wall Street valued Peloton on recurring software subscription multiples, believing the hardware was merely an upfront customer acquisition funnel. In reality, when shipping costs spiked and bike prices were discounted to clear warehouses, hardware gross margin collapsed from +43.1% to -11.2% (and -98.1% in Q4 FY22). Every new customer acquired widened the balance sheet deficit.
3. **The Secondary-Market Cannibalization Trap**: Exercise bikes have a 7–10 year durable-goods lifespan. Once affluent early-adopters were equipped, thousands of gently-used bikes flooded eBay, Facebook Marketplace, and Craigslist at 75% discounts. Peloton had to onboard, stream video to, and service these second-hand users while receiving $0 in hardware revenue—eventually forcing the defensive August 2024 $95 "Used Equipment Activation Fee."
4. **Adversarial Regulatory Hubris**: The Tread+ safety crisis was compounded by corporate obstinance. When the CPSC alerted the public to pediatric entrapments beneath the unshielded 59-slat motorized track, Peloton publicly attacked federal regulators as "inaccurate and misleading"—only to apologize 18 days later, issue a mandatory recall of 125,000 units, and later settle a record $19.065M civil penalty for failing to report 150+ incidents and continuing to distribute recalled units.
5. **Dual-Class Governance Shield**: Insider Class B shares carried 20 votes per share versus 1 vote for Class A public shares, concentrating 80% of voting power among John Foley and co-founders, leaving outside institutional capital powerless until cash burn exceeded $2.4 billion in a single year.

## 3. The Pivotal Detail
*The single most load-bearing document and physical artifact:*
- **The $33M Fire-Sale of the $400M Peloton Output Park (POP)**: In May 2021, Peloton broke ground on a 1.2M sq. ft. manufacturing facility on 200 acres in Troy Township, Wood County, Ohio, designed to produce 1.5M bikes annually and employ 2,000 workers. Halted nine months later in February 2022, the abandoned structural steel skeleton sat vacant for nearly two years until First Solar bought the entire site in January 2024 for just $33 million—a catastrophic >70% capital destruction on a single asset.

## 5. Verified Factual Spine & Timeline

- **September 2019**: Peloton goes public on NASDAQ at ~$29/share (~$7B valuation). Dual-class governance structure grants 20 votes per Class B share to insiders.
- **November 2019**: "The Gift That Gives Back" holiday commercial goes viral for marital pressure and elitism; stock loses $1.5B in market value over several sessions.
- **March 2020 – December 2020**: COVID-19 lockdowns shutter gyms. Monthly workouts surge to 24.7 per user; delivery backlogs stretch past 10 weeks. Market cap peaks above **$50 Billion** (>$160/share). Hardware gross margins reach **+43.1%**.
- **December 2020**: Peloton pays **$420 Million in cash** to acquire commercial fitness vendor Precor for domestic manufacturing capacity.
- **Q2–Q3 FY 2021**: Company expends **~$100 Million** in chartered transpacific air freight to bypass congested shipping ports.
- **March–April 2021**: A 6-year-old child dies and dozens of injuries are reported from Tread+ rear-roller entrapment. CPSC issues urgent administrative warning on April 17; Peloton issues corporate release calling the warning "inaccurate and misleading."
- **May 5, 2021**: CEO John Foley publicly apologizes, issuing voluntary recall of ~125,000 Tread+ units ($26.5M immediate revenue write-down).
- **May 2021**: Peloton breaks ground on the **$400 Million, 1.2M sq. ft. Peloton Output Park** in Troy Township, Wood County, Ohio.
- **December 9–16, 2021**: *And Just Like That...* airs Mr. Big's fatal heart attack on a Peloton Bike+. Stock drops 11.35%. Ryan Reynolds / Maximum Effort releases 48-hour parody spot ("He's alive"), which is pulled days later following sexual assault allegations against actor Chris Noth.
- **January 24, 2022**: Activist Blackwells Capital issues 65-page presentation demanding John Foley's removal and exploring a sale of the business.
- **February 8, 2022**: Foley steps down as CEO. Board brings in former Netflix/Spotify CFO Barry McCarthy. 2,800 jobs cut; construction on the Ohio factory halted immediately.
- **June 30, 2022 (FY 2022 Close)**: Annual net loss reaches **$2.83 Billion**. Connected Fitness hardware gross margin collapses to **-11.2%** (reaching **-98.1%** in Q4). Inventory write-downs total **$337 Million**. Free cash burn for the year hits **-$2.46 Billion**.
- **January 5, 2023**: CPSC announces a **$19.065 Million civil penalty** against Peloton for knowingly failing to report Tread+ entrapments and distributing 38 recalled units.
- **January 2024**: First Solar acquires the unfinished 1.2M sq. ft. Ohio factory shell and 200 acres for **$33 Million** (pennies on the invested dollar).
- **May 2, 2024**: Barry McCarthy resigns after slashing operating cash burn from -$2.46B to -$125M; announces an additional 15% workforce reduction.
- **August 2024**: Peloton introduces a mandatory **$95 Used Equipment Activation Fee** for secondary-market buyers to stem dilution.
- **October 31, 2024**: Apple Fitness+ co-founder Peter Stern named CEO and President, effective January 1, 2025.
