## Pass 2 — Check

**Zero-context gut check** (would a stranger scrolling past tap it, without knowing anything?):
- **Cut 4** ("The Queue That Never Moves"). It only makes sense after watching.
- **Cut 12.** "It's not X" in brackets is overused, and the brackets add nothing.
- **Cut 8.** The "POV" wording fits the personal Draft B, not A's world tour.
- **Cut 10.** "Twice as long" is vaguer than a hard number.
- **Cut 9.** The joke is too long to read at a glance, and it gives away the twist.
- **Keep 1.** It's the plainest statement of the premise and searchable. The strangeness is in the premise itself.
- **Keep 7.** It shifts the question onto the viewer's own family. "Why *my parents*?" is the curiosity gap, and the video pays it off.
- **Keep 3 and 5.** Each adds one concrete, surprising consequence.
- **Keep 6.** It's the same premise from the death side. Punchier, but slightly darker.

## Hook (first 5 seconds)
- **Opening line:** "Imagine a birthday party. The girl is six. There are forty people in the room, and thirty of them are her parents, her grandparents, and the grandparents of her grandparents."
- **Opening image (AI video, cold open):** a bright living-room birthday party, balloons, a small girl at a cake. The camera drifts back to reveal the room packed with young-looking adults, then lands on a smiling man bouncing on a bouncy castle with an "I'm 126!" badge.
- **Open loop (by ~0:25):** "By the end, you'll see why, in this world, the healthiest mother on the street is her children's biggest problem."
