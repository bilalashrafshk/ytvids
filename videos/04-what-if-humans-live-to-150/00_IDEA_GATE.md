## IDEA GATE
CHANNEL: FinanceCraft
WORKING TITLE: What If Humans Lived to 150?
THESIS: Living to 150 wouldn't give you a longer retirement. It would break everything built for an 80-year life: careers, marriage, inheritance, housing and who holds power.
DEMAND: NOT SUPPLIED
SHAPE: c1 — World what-if

*Premise as drafted (engine assumption, confirm at HARD STOP 0): ageing slows for everyone, so people stay healthy and working-age far longer and die around 150. Not "old for 70 extra years".*

### Q1 PRIOR — PASS
Viewer believes: living longer just means more years of being old, added on at the end — a longer retirement.

### Q2 UNGUESSABLE — PASS
Viewer guess: lots of very old people, overcrowding, and pensions going broke.
Actual: the extra years land in the middle of life, so you'd stay young for a century — and the real shock is who never lets go of jobs, homes, money and power.

### Q3 EVERYDAY ENTRY — PASS
Opens on: a family dinner where your great-great-grandmother is still at work on Monday, and your parents, in their eighties, are still waiting to inherit her house.

### Q4 MECHANISM — PASS
Almost everything in life is timed for an 80-year span: a 40-year career, a house passed on in your fifties, "till death do us part". Stretch life to 150 and each of those timers breaks.

### Q5 ESCALATION — PASS
1. Day one: nothing visible happens — people just stop getting older (your body)
2. A 40-year career becomes a 100-year one; retiring at 65 stops making sense (your job)
3. Parents hand down the house at 120, so homes stay locked with the oldest (where you live)
4. "Till death do us part" now means 120 years together (your family)
5. The same people run companies and countries for a century (who's in charge)
6. Births have to fall hard, or the planet fills up (everyone)

### Q6 MOVING NUMBER — PASS
Number: the age you'd be when you inherit your parents' home — somewhere in your fifties today, climbing past 100.

### Q7 CAMERA TEST — PASS
- A birthday cake with 150 candles and a smoke alarm going off
- A wedding ring engraved "until 2140"
- A retirement gold watch still in its box, never given
- A 130-year-old in a suit at a job interview
- A family photo with six living generations squeezed onto one sofa
- A "For Sale" sign that never goes up on the same house for a hundred years

### Q8 COMPLICATION — PASS
Counterpoint: longer lives have always made the world richer — more experience, more time to build things — so this could be pure upside.
Enters at: right after the "who's in charge" section, as the turn: the upside is real, but only if the old are willing to step aside.

### Q9 CLOSING LINE — PASS
We already live about twice as long as people did 150 years ago, and we never redesigned anything for it. The question isn't whether we'd get the extra years. It's whether we'd change anything.

### Q10 LANE FIT — PASS
Track: 2 — the real mechanics that must hold: savings growing over a far longer life, the timing of inheritance, and the population sum of births against deaths. Money is one lens in the tour, not the whole video.

VERDICT: PASS
BIGGEST RISK: Demand is unproven and big channels have already covered ageing, so the video lives or dies on the "who never lets go" angle, not the premise itself.
