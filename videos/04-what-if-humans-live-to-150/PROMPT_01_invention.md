Rough premise: **What if humans lived to 150? Ageing slows for everyone, so people stay young and healthy far longer and die around 150 — not 70 extra years of being old**. ## Register

## The discipline this version adds: don't let the chain wander off the premise

Every beat below has to still be visibly about **living to 150** to a first-time viewer. It's easy to follow a mechanism to its most technically interesting place and end up making a video about something else entirely wearing this premise's title — a "what if the feeds went dark" video that becomes, by the midpoint, a video about port demurrage schedules and OAuth token lifecycles. That happened here before. The fix isn't to avoid going deep — the $1 Trillion benchmark goes deep into yachts, grain markets, and stock exchanges — it's that every domain it visits is still, transparently, about *spending the money*. The throughline never breaks even as the domains change. Hold your chains to that same test: **does this beat still feel like it's about living to 150, or has living to 150 become a forgotten trigger for a different topic?**

## PART 1 — Five premises, not one

Give me five versions of this scenario, and make at least two of them uncomfortable — past the point of reasonable. For each, one line only.

Use these angles:
1. **The constraint twist** — add a rule that makes the obvious path impossible
2. **The wrong protagonist** — the position nobody picks to tell it from
3. **The inversion** — the recovery instead of the collapse; the person who called it early and got punished
4. **The absurd literalisation** — take the abstraction and give it physical weight and a location
5. **The compressed clock** — force a decade into a week, or stretch an instant across a year

Then: which is strongest, and one sentence on why each of the other four is weaker.

## PART 2 — The contract (Draft B / A5 only)

*Answer this for Draft B only. Draft A (A12) has no protagonist or adversary and uses the Cascade kit in Part 8.*

The scenario's rules, stated the way a legal notice would state them:
- What just happened (one sentence)
- The clock — how long
- The penalty — what is lost when it runs out
- The rules — what is and isn't allowed inside the scenario
- **The adversary** — the thing working against the protagonist *while they act*. Compounding interest, spreading failure, a closing window. It must be expressible as a number that moves.

All five must fit in 80 spoken words. Write them that way.

**If this is a countdown/ledger format (A5), give me the reconciled numbers now, not later.** State the adversary's rate plainly (e.g. "$X burned per day"), then compute the running figure at 3-4 checkpoints across the scenario's clock **by that rate**, showing the arithmetic (`start − rate × days = figure`). Every checkpoint must actually follow from the stated rate. A ledger with numbers that don't reconcile with their own stated cause is the single most common failure in this format, and it's a research failure, not a scripting one — it has to be caught here.

## PART 4 — Third-order consequences

For the four biggest beats, push the chain three steps past the obvious:

> first order (what everyone predicts) → second order → **third order (the beat worth filming), and the one-line test: how would a viewer still know this is about living to 150?**

Worked example, staying on-theme throughout (the premise: a sudden ban on a country's cash currency):
*people can't pay for anything in cash → shopkeepers refuse goods without an app they don't have → a street vendor invents a paper IOU system scrawled in a school notebook, backed by nothing but her own name, because the thing missing was never the money, it was a way to promise it.*
Every step of that chain is still recognizably about *paying for things*. Compare the failure version: a premise about paying for things drifting into a chain about warehouse authentication protocols and shipping-container law — technically downstream, but the viewer has stopped watching a video about payment three steps ago.

Stop at first order and I have a list of things the viewer already assumed. I want scenes. But stop the chain the moment it stops being about living to 150, even if a more interesting mechanism is one step further down it — save that mechanism for a different episode with its own premise.
