## Pass 2 — Check

**Zero-context gut check** (would a stranger tap it, knowing nothing about the video?):

**The working title (#1) fails four separate checks. It's your call to keep, but here is what a cold viewer or a fact-checking commenter will hit:**
- **"Monopoly."** The script itself tells viewers that a federal judge found the contract did not hurt competition. A title that says "monopoly" invites the top comment "the court said it isn't".
- **"Tax."** It isn't a tax. It's a private fee. A commenter will say so, and the video's own point is that nobody voted on it.
- **"Every Screen on Earth."** Not every screen has HDMI, and not every one pays the fee in the same way. It's an overclaim the script deliberately avoids.
- **"$0.04."** That is the lowest of three tiers ($0.15, $0.05, $0.04), not the price. "$0.04" as a dollar figure also reads oddly at a glance. "4-cent" reads instantly.
- The subject also arrives late: "HDMI" is the first word but "Monopoly" is the hook, and it's the word the video argues with.

**The rest:**
- **Cut 3.** "Pay a Fee to Plug in a Cable" implies cable buyers pay per cable, which the research doesn't support.
- **Cut 4.** "Has an Owner" is a claim with no number and nothing to picture.
- **Cut 7.** "Tiny fee" is vague and there's no reason to tap.
- **Cut 8.** "Is Not Free" is the "it's not X" construction, and "Here's Who Collects" gives the game away as a list.
- **Cut 11.** No number, no stakes.
- **Cut 12.** "Took over every living room" is a big claim with no fee to anchor it, and "how" promises a history lecture.
- **Cut 13.** "Costs somebody" is vague.
- **Cut 14.** It gives the answer away (lost, paid).
- **Keep 2, 5, 6, 9, 10.** Each has one concrete, picturable thing in the first four words: a 4-cent toll, a plug, a search term, a dollar figure, a small TV maker.

**Accuracy notes per title (so nothing gets challenged in the comments):**
- **#1:** "4-cent" is the lowest tier for a device with the logo and the lock. The video explains the tiers by about 4:20, and the first line of the description says "as low as four cents". "Toll" is a metaphor the script uses on purpose. Risk: a viewer who thinks the fee is always 4 cents. Mitigation is in the script.
- **#2:** "Gets Paid" is fine: HDMI LA collects on each device. It's paid when the device is made, not when you plug it in, so the title is a hook, not a literal. Low risk.
- **#3:** "Who owns HDMI?" is what people ask; the honest answer in the video is "a licensing agent for the founders". A short, direct answer exists, so this is a search-magnet.
- **#4:** The $14 million is real and sourced. It is a fight *about* HDMI, so the title is literal.
- **#5:** "Pay more" rests on our own arithmetic from the reported fees. It appears in the video labelled as our own math; in the title it has no number, so it can't be challenged as a precise claim.

**Recommended: #1 as the launch title, A/B tested against #2.** #1 is the closest to your working title in spirit (a number plus a hidden fee) but drops the three claims the video contradicts. #2 is the stronger click if #1 tests as too abstract. #3 is the search play. The final pick is yours.

**If you want to keep "HDMI" in the title with the number:** "The 4-Cent HDMI Toll Inside Every TV" is the closest version of your original that avoids "monopoly", "tax", "$0.04" and "every screen on earth".

## Core curiosity gap

*"You've plugged in that cable a thousand times, and you've never once seen the bill. Somebody has. Who, how much, and why can't anyone say no?"*

The gap is **who collects**, **how much**, and **why nobody can skip it**. The video pays all three off in the first ten minutes, and saves the last (the AMD driver) for the end.

## Opening hook (first 5 seconds)

- **Opening line (VO):** "Tilt your head and look at the back of your TV."
- **Opening image (AI video, cold open):** a living-room TV on a stand. The camera glides round to the back. A hand pushes a cable into a small flat port. A coin drops onto the screen, then a price tag reading "4¢" folds out of the port.
- **Open loop (by ~0:25):** "This video follows one chain of cause and effect. Each link forces the next. By the end, you'll see why the four cents is the smallest thing about it."

## Provisional description hook (first two lines, shown before "show more")

> Every TV, console and monitor with an HDMI port carries a fee that can be as low as four cents. Here's who collects it, why nobody can skip it, and what happened when one company tried.

*Expanding paragraph (2–4 sentences):* In 2002, seven electronics rivals built one plug to replace a knot of cables. They added a copy-protection lock to win over the film studios, and a contract to pay for it. Twenty-three years and about fourteen billion devices later, the plug is on almost everything you own. This video follows the chain of cause and effect that made it impossible to avoid, from the founding to a federal court in 2025 to an AMD driver that Linux users still can't have.

*Note on our own numbers:* the total-money range and the small-maker comparison in the video are our own arithmetic on the widely reported fee schedule, not published figures. The description should say so in one line.
