### Editorial Recommendation
**Recommended Title:** **What If Facebook and Instagram Went Dark for 30 Days?** (or **Facebook Charged $42 to Sell a $1.10 Product** for A/B testing).
- *Rationale (revised after editorial pushback on layman appeal):* The prior locked title ("POV: A Warehouse Owner During the 30-Day Blackout") and its A/B partner ("The $42 Ad Click 6,000 Companies Depended On") both passed the mechanical Familiar Anchor Rule and fiction-signal check, but neither passes a plain "would a random viewer with zero context get this in one second" test — "POV" is a genre convention this format doesn't use, and "$42 ad click" presumes the viewer already understands ad-auction economics before they've clicked. The new master title needs no prior knowledge: everyone knows Facebook and Instagram, and "went dark" is self-explanatory. It satisfies the Familiar Anchor Rule ("Facebook" in word three) and the Track 2 fiction signal ("What If" + question mark). The $42/$1.10 alt is kept as the numeric-shock A/B variant because the contrast is self-explanatory on its own (no "ad auction" terminology needed) even without a fiction signal.
- **Packaging lock:** Whichever of these two ships is the *only* title/hook combination used across `04_THUMBNAILS.md` and `13_FINAL_METADATA.md` — see Section 10's "packaging locks to one draft" rule.

## 3. Provisional Narrative Hook & Evidentiary Citations (Pre-Production)

### Pre-Production Video Hook & Description Draft
Forty refrigerated shipping containers are leaking melted butter onto the asphalt at the Port of Los Angeles. Every social media feed is dark, but the real catastrophe isn't on your phone screen—it's locked behind the terminal gates.

When every major social media platform goes dark for thirty days, popular commentary predicts a peaceful cultural renaissance and a surge in workplace focus. In reality, modern commerce is not a physical marketplace; it is an ad auction masquerading as a retail store. The moment algorithmic customer discovery vanishes, direct-to-consumer cash flow freezes instantly, turning millions of dollars of physical inventory into abandoned toxic waste, trapping global truck chassis, and triggering mass corporate defaults while the world celebrates a quiet offline vacation.
