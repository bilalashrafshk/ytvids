# Episode 03 Research Brief: Social Media Blackout for 30 Days
## An Operational and Macroeconomic Thought Experiment

> **Evidentiary Spine & Concept Input**: This document establishes the single source of truth for the fictional scenario, operational mechanics, verified math, physical objects, and third-order consequence chains for Episode 03 of FinanceCraft.

---

## PART 1 — Five Premises

* **The constraint twist:** The platforms are dark, but your programmatic ad accounts keep running on cached server clocks, debiting merchant checking accounts daily for phantom clicks until balances hit zero.
* **The wrong protagonist:** A customs bonded warehouse receiver in Long Beach who cannot legally release thousands of containers because the bill-of-lading confirmation links are locked behind dead "Log in with Facebook" portals.
* **The inversion:** A media buyer shorts Meta and liquidates inventory on day two, but gets margin-called and ruined on day twenty-nine by an emergency private bailout vehicle funded by logistics cartels.
* **The absurd literalisation:** Subsea fiber cables remain lit, but all transatlantic packet headers resolving to five specific corporate ASN blocks are dumped into heat sinks at a single switching vault in Slough, England, boiling the cooling loop dry.
* **The compressed clock:** The platforms are only down for seventy-two hours, but payments settle on net-30, so the absolute cash death of six thousand brands arrives silently four weeks later on a bright Tuesday morning.

**The strongest:** The customs bonded warehouse receiver in Long Beach.

* **Constraint twist:** Weaker because ghost billing is an administrative bank dispute solved with a phone call to stop payment, not a physical crisis.
* **Inversion:** Weaker because financial short-seller mechanics distance the audience from physical reality and turn the narrative into an options-trading explainer.
* **Absurd literalisation:** Weaker because focusing on server hardware turns the episode into network infrastructure disaster porn rather than an operational trade collapse.
* **Compressed clock:** Weaker because delayed lag-time confuses dramatic tension on screen; viewers lose the sensory link between the server crash and the bankruptcy filing.

## PART 2 — The Contract

Every Tier-1 consumer platform has severed connections globally. You have thirty days until automated net-30 vendor invoices draft from empty operating accounts. Failure triggers collateral seizure, warehouse evictions, and personal debt guarantees. You may use landlines, SMS, and paper checks; you cannot reroute traffic to new domains without triggering bank anti-fraud freezes. The adversary is days sales of inventory divided by cash burn: a daily burn rate of $18,400 moving against a ledger balance dropping toward zero.

## PART 4 — Third-Order Consequences

### Beat 1: Direct-to-Consumer Customer Acquisition Dies

* **First order:** Brands stop running targeted ads; incoming online store sales fall by 92% within 48 hours.
* **Second order:** Automatic daily sweeps from merchant processors fail; digital lenders like Clearco and Wayflyer freeze existing credit lines and demand instant principal paydowns.
* **Third order:** Founders who signed personal guarantees on merchant cash advances find their personal residential mortgages encumbered by statutory UCC liens before day fourteen.

### Beat 2: Single Sign-On (SSO) and OAuth Rails Go Dark

* **First order:** Employees cannot log in to third-party SaaS tools like Gorgias, Zendesk, or Klaviyo because the "Sign in with Google/Facebook" authentication server returns an HTTP 504 error.
* **Second order:** Automated inventory sync tools stop communicating with warehouse management systems; inventory counts freeze at the third-party logistics center.
* **Third order:** Warehouse managers, unable to verify who owns which skid without software access, legally classify entire facilities as abandoned cargo and lock the bay doors to protect themselves from insurance fraud claims.

### Beat 3: Emerging-Market Informal Trade Halts

* **First order:** Family-run micro-retailers in Southeast Asia and Latin America lose customer chats, catalog views, and pickup coordination managed exclusively through WhatsApp Business.
* **Second order:** Cash flow dries up instantly; shop owners fail to settle informal credit balances with local wholesale supply trucks that arrive on Tuesday mornings.
* **Third order:** Wholesale food distributors refuse to unload perishable perishables on credit, diverting shipments to municipal incinerators to maintain price floors while neighborhood market stalls sit empty.

### Beat 4: Port Congestion and Working Capital Collapse

* **First order:** Cargo ships arrive carrying consumer goods pre-ordered three months prior; importers do not pick them up because consumer demand evaporated.
* **Second order:** Containers sit on chassis; the ocean carriers bill shippers compounding demurrage and detention fees directly against their operating letters of credit.
* **Third order:** Local trucking fleets run out of bare chassis because thousands of them are trapped acting as makeshift, unpaid storage units in parking lots, which halts grain exports moving the opposite direction from the interior.

## PART 5 — The Mechanic, Verified

### The Mathematical Reality

The math behind an ad-driven cash conversion cycle ($CCC$) behaves as:

$$CCC = DIO + DSO - DPO$$

* $DIO$ = Days Inventory Outstanding (typically 60 to 90 days for imported consumer goods).
* $DSO$ = Days Sales Outstanding (0 to 2 days on credit card rails).
* $DPO$ = Days Payables Outstanding (typically 30 to 60 days on factory purchase orders, but 0 to 30 days on ad networks).

In normal direct-to-consumer operations, programmatic acquisition maintains an artificial state of high liquidity: incoming credit card cash ($DSO \approx 1$) pays for ongoing ad spend and covers older inventory payables ($DPO \approx 45$).

When traffic drops to zero instantly:

1. Inflow drops to zero, but $DPO$ clocks continue to run down.
2. In-transit inventory ($DIO$) cannot be liquidated at any discount without customer discovery channels.
3. Fixed obligations mature on a 30-day staggered cycle.

```
Day 0: Cash $150,000 | Receivables $0 | Unpaid Inventory POs (due Day 20) $180,000 | Container Fees $0/day
Day 10: Cash $110,000 | Daily Ad Spend $0 | Fixed Overhead $40,000 | Container Fees $2,750/day
Day 20: Cash $35,000  | Inventory PO Due: $180,000 -> DEFAULT ($145,000 shortfall)
Day 30: Cash -$47,500 | Incurred Demurrage: $82,500 | Bank sweeps remaining accounts | Insolvency
```

### Real Anchor Figures

* **Ocean Carrier Demurrage/Detention:** Ports typically grant 4 to 5 "free days" after container discharge. After free time expires, standard port and carrier penalties average $150 to $350 per container per day for dry boxes, and $300 to $600 per day for refrigerated containers, scaling upward every five days.
* **OAuth Token Lifespans:** Refresh tokens for Google and Meta enterprise services carry hard expirations. Standard OAuth access tokens expire every 60 minutes; refresh tokens can last months, but if the identity provider's revocation endpoint or public key infrastructure (JWKS) cannot be contacted to revalidate signing keys, downstream enterprise systems revoke local session tickets within 24 to 72 hours of failed connection.
* **Working Capital Ratios:** The median venture-backed DTC brand operates with 1.5 to 2.5 months of cash runway. Their inventory turnover ratio is usually between 3.0 and 4.0, meaning inventory converts to cash roughly every 90 to 120 days.

### Where the Premise Gets the Mechanic Wrong

The premise implies that when social platforms go down, the core point-of-sale or database systems collapse directly. They do not.

* **The Shopify/Stripe Layer Still Works:** A retailer's website hosted on independent infrastructure (AWS, Cloudflare) stays up. Their Stripe checkout stays open. The system failure is not transactional; it is pure customer acquisition starve-out.
* **The Email Channel Does Not Die:** Real brands retain customer lists on Klaviyo or Mailchimp. Brands with large owned-audience lists (first-party customer data) survive on email blasts for 10 to 14 days before discounting exhausts repeat buyers. The blackout only instantly strangles companies that rely entirely on cold paid traffic from Meta and TikTok ad auctions.
* **OAuth Workarounds Exist:** For critical systems, software administrators do not sit paralyzed for 30 days; they deploy password-reset fallback overrides to circumvent social logins within 48 hours, unless corporate policies have strictly prohibited direct password generation. The realistic failure occurs where companies use social sign-in as their *only* recovery email infrastructure.

## PART 7 — Where This Gets Boring

* **The "Influencer Panic" Trap:** The viewer expects montage sequences of vanity creators crying because they cannot post selfies. If you show an influencer staring at a black screen, the audience checks out; it feels shallow and dated. Cut all creators entirely.
* **The "Cyber Mystery" Subplot:** Viewers will spend the first ten minutes waiting for you to explain *why* the platforms are down—asking if it was Russia, an EMP, or a rogue AI. If you feed that mystery, you lose. Treat the blackout like a rainstorm: an uninteresting, permanent physical condition. The drama is the water rising in the basement.
* **The "Barter Economy" Cliché:** Writers always push trade disruption into post-apocalyptic barter within three weeks (people trading gold watches for batteries). Financial collapse in modern trade looks like spreadsheets full of unpaid demurrage charges and dead corporate credit cards, not people trading sacks of wheat on street corners. Keep it anchored in commercial invoices, locked chain-link gates, and legal notices.
