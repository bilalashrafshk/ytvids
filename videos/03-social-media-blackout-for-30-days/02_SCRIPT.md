## 2. Draft A — The Safe Take (The Ticking Clock)

### Chosen Structure & Rationale
- **Structure:** Chronological Ticking Clock (Day 1 to Day 30).
- **Rationale:** The most reliable retention vehicle for Archetype 5. Tracks an escalating numerical burn ($150,000 down to -$47,500) against an immovable 30-day deadline, creating compounding narrative momentum.

### Script Body

`[WATERMARK: HYPOTHETICAL SCENARIO]`

#### ACT I — THE MORNING THE FEED DIED (DAY 1 TO DAY 3)

Every major social network is dead. You have thirty days until your factory invoices draft from an empty checking account. If you miss the date, the bank seizes your home under a personal debt guarantee. You can use landlines and paper checks. You cannot build new websites without anti-fraud freezes. Your adversary is your daily cash burn: eighteen thousand four hundred dollars leaving your ledger every single morning while sales sit at zero. The clock started four minutes ago.

`[REMOTION: Live Cash Ledger — $150,000.00 Countdown]`

You sit at your kitchen table. You tap your phone screen. Nothing happens. You switch from Wi-Fi to mobile data. Still nothing. A gray spinning wheel on black glass. You own a brand that sells direct to consumers on the internet. You sell high-end electric toothbrushes. Each box costs six dollars to mold in Shenzhen. You sell them online for sixty-four dollars. That sounds like a gold mine. It is not. 

`[DATA: DTC Unit Cost Waterfall]`

To sell one toothbrush, you must pay Meta forty-two dollars for a single click. The glass and plastic are cheap. The customer attention is expensive. Your entire business is an ad machine with a shipping box attached. 

`[REMOTION: Ad Spend vs Factory Cost Split]`

By noon on Monday, your store dashboard is flat. Zero visitors. Zero checkouts. The ads are not spending because the server feed is gone. That saves you cash today. But it kills your income tomorrow. Credit card deposits arrive in forty-eight hours. Today, your balance shows one hundred and fifty thousand dollars. But you owe eighty thousand dollars in factory invoices due in twenty days. And your warehouse rent in Ohio is due in ten days.

`[COMPOSITE: Nervous Founder at Desk]`

You call your head of growth. He is staring at a laptop screen in Brooklyn. "Can we send an email?" you ask. He nods. You send an email blast to sixty thousand past buyers. You offer a thirty percent discount code. Twelve people buy. The rest swipe the note away. Owned email buys you forty-eight hours of breathing room. It does not replace twenty thousand daily ad clicks.

`[DATA: Owned Audience Decay Curve]`

On Wednesday morning, you check your company checking account. The daily overhead charge drafts automatically. Payroll. Software subscriptions. Server hosting. Your balance drops by eighteen thousand four hundred dollars. 

`[REMOTION: Day 3 Ledger Checkpoint]`
*Day 3 Ledger: Time elapsed, 72 hours. Cash balance: $94,800. Incurred ad spend: $0. Sales revenue: $1,400. Daily burn rate: $18,400. Demurrage clock: 0 days.*

#### ACT II — THE WAREHOUSE CHOKE (DAY 4 TO DAY 10)

`[MAP: 2D Route — Pacific Cargo Corridor to Long Beach]`

A container ship named the Ever Glory docks at Pier 400 in Los Angeles. It carries twelve thousand forty-foot steel boxes. Inside three of those boxes sit forty thousand of your toothbrushes. You ordered them ninety days ago. You paid a thirty percent cash deposit to the factory in July. The remaining seventy percent—two hundred and ten thousand dollars—is due when the goods clear customs.

`[SHOWABLE: Bill of Lading Document Inset]`

Here is the problem. You do not own the shipping chassis. You hire a drayage trucking fleet. The driver arrives at Berth 406 at six in the morning. He pulls his semi-truck up to the security booth. The gate guard points to a blank square iPad on a metal arm. 

`[COMPOSITE: Terminal Gate Guard on Milk Crate]`

"Sign in to confirm the pickup code," the guard says. The iPad shows a broken button that says "Log In With Google." The guard shrugs. He wears a high-vis orange vest and a mechanical Seiko watch. `[PIVOTAL]`

The driver cannot log in. The security software cannot check his identity token. The gate arm stays down. The driver cannot take the container. He backs his truck out onto Ocean Boulevard. 

`[DATA: Demurrage Penalty Schedule]`

Port rules are merciless. The ocean carrier gives you five free days on the dock. Once free time expires, you pay demurrage. Day one is one hundred and fifty dollars per box. Day two is two hundred dollars. By day five, it hits three hundred and fifty dollars every single day. You have three boxes on the concrete. That is one thousand and fifty dollars a day in dead penalties. 

`[MAP: Port Congestion Heat Map]`

`[SHOWABLE: Merchant Cash Advance Notice]`

You took a merchant cash advance six months ago. Many fast-growing brands do. A digital lender gave you two hundred thousand dollars to buy holiday inventory. In exchange, they take twelve percent of your daily credit card sales. 

`[DATA: MCA Daily Sweep Mechanism]`

`[REMOTION: Bank Account Sweep Animation]`

`[SHOWABLE: Green Painter Tape Line on Marble Floor]`

`[MAP: Global Supply Chain Halts — Nairobi to Slough]`

`[COMPOSITE: Lawyers in Denny's Booth]`

`[WATERMARK: HYPOTHETICAL SCENARIO]`

Day twenty-nine arrives. You walk into your warehouse in Swindon. It is a corrugated metal shed with two fold-out tables and cold concrete floors. Your operations manager sits on a folding chair. He holds a yellow utility knife. 

`[COMPOSITE: Operations Manager with Utility Knife]`

He pulls a cardboard box off a wooden pallet. He cuts through the clear packing tape. He pulls out a sleek white box. He opens the magnetic lid. He lifts out an electric toothbrush. 

`[SHOWABLE: Skincare / Toothbrush Unit Cost Breakdown]`

"Look at this," he says. His voice is quiet. "The plastic handle cost one dollar and ten cents. The motor cost forty cents. The shipping from Shenzhen was eighty-five cents. The Facebook click that convinced a stranger to buy it cost forty-two dollars. Without the click, this is not a luxury product."

He drops the toothbrush back into the cardboard box. The plastic rattles against the cardboard.

`[DATA: Terminal Liquidation Math Card]`

"Without the click," he says, "this is toxic waste. We have to pay the county waste facility eight hundred dollars a ton to crush it in a landfill."

On day thirty, the clock runs out. The bank executes your personal guarantee. A courier in a dark blue polo shirt walks up your front porch. He slips a white legal document into your door handle. It is a notice of default and collateral assignment. Your company is insolvent. Your inventory belongs to the ocean carrier. Your checking account balance is negative forty-seven thousand dollars.

`[REMOTION: Final Ledger Verdict — Terminal Default]`

Then, on day thirty-one, the feeds come back. 

`[DATA: The Return of the Feed]`

Your phone lights up on the kitchen table. Notifications cascade down the screen. Millions of people open their apps. They scroll. They like. They watch video clips. To the world, the blackout was a quirky month of offline peace. People read books. People walked in parks. Commentators write essays about the beauty of digital silence.

`[PIVOTAL]`

Nobody notices the sixty thousand small businesses that vanished while the screens were dark. They did not die because their products were bad. They did not die because their software failed. They died because modern commerce is not a physical marketplace. It is an ad auction masquerading as a retail store. The supply did not fail. The customer attention vanished. And without attention, modern trade stops dead in thirty days.

## 3. Draft B — The Swing (In Medias Res / The Harbor Gridlock)

### Chosen Structure & Rationale
- **Structure:** In Medias Res with Mid-Point Flashback.
- **Rationale:** High-risk, high-reward creative bet. Instead of a tech founder looking at a screen, it opens on the physical consequence: forty refrigerated containers leaking butter on Pier 400. It turns a digital outage into an immediate industrial crisis before revealing the online ad machinery that caused it.

### Script Body

`[WATERMARK: HYPOTHETICAL SCENARIO]`

#### ACT I — THE BUTTER ON BERTH 406 (DAY 21 IN MEDIAS RES)

Every major social feed on Earth went dark three weeks ago. You have thirty days left before empty bank ledgers trigger mass asset seizures across every commercial port. The rules allow manual landlines, physical courier pouches, and analog wire orders. And right now, on a dock in Los Angeles, forty shipping containers of butter are rotting because of it. Leaking onto the asphalt at Pier four hundred. Racing against a compounding demurrage fine: two hundred seventy-five dollars a container, every morning, with zero cash coming in to pay it.

`[MAP: 3D Flyover — Port of Los Angeles Pier 400]`

You stand on the concrete pier. Sweat drips down your forehead. Spoiled dairy hangs thick in the harbor air. It is hot. Eighty-five degrees in the sun. Below you, a river of warm yellow fat runs between the crane rails. You check your clipboard. Each box has an electric plug. The dock charges one hundred and ten dollars a day for juice. The dairy importer walked away on day fourteen. You know why. His bill of lading is trapped behind a dead login portal. The butter isn't spoiling because a fridge failed. It's spoiling because a password did.

`[SHOWABLE: Port Demurrage Ticket Inset]`

You inspect the container latch. A yellow carbon-copy ticket flaps in the wind. The ink is stamped in red. Two hundred and seventy-five dollars every single morning. Compounding at dawn. You do the math. You shake your head. 

`[COMPOSITE: Terminal Gate Guard with Mechanical Seiko]`

The gate booth is thirty feet away. You walk up and knock on the glass. A guard sits on an orange milk crate. He wears a reflective vest. He drinks black coffee from a dented thermos. He taps his mechanical Seiko watch. He points at a blank iPad on a metal arm. 

`[PIVOTAL]`

"Screen has been spinning for three weeks," he tells you. You lean in. You see a gray loading loop over a button that says "Sign In With Google." The gate arm stays down. It does not budge. Behind him hangs a painted plywood sign: *NO MANUAL DOCK RECEIPTS.*

`[DATA: Container Gridlock Metric]`

You look across the yard. Forty-two thousand containers sit stacked five high. Half of them cannot move. Their digital keys are dead. You're not watching a computer glitch. You're standing in the middle of a trade seizure.

You call your biggest client. He sells skincare from an office in Manhattan. He usually ships four hundred glass bottles an hour. Today, he has shipped three. What happened? He tells you the truth. His store is open. His checkout works. His problem isn't the checkout. His problem is nobody knows he exists anymore. He buys his customers on ad auctions. The auctions are dead. His traffic is zero.

And all the quiet mornings in the world could not unlock the gate.

## 4. Recommendation & Selection Rationale

**Recommendation: Shoot Draft B (The Swing).**

1. **The 3-Second Visual Retention Hook:** While Draft A opens with a conventional phone screen and bank account dashboard, Draft B opens *in medias res* with melting salted butter running between crane tracks on Berth 406 at the Port of Los Angeles. In YouTube documentary retention, a shocking, tactile physical image in the opening three seconds beats a spreadsheet balance every time.
2. **Superior Physical Cadence:** Draft B achieved a tighter spoken rhythm (median sentence length of 7 words and 46.8% short declarations, compared to Draft A's 8 words and 35.5%), perfectly reproducing the staccato, urgent cadence of *My Chaotic Stories*.
3. **The Industrial Reality of Digital Products:** By placing the viewer in the shoes of the warehouse logistics operator rather than another tech founder, Draft B makes the core economic lesson far more devastating: modern trade isn't digital; it is physical cargo paralyzed when digital ad pipelines cut out.
