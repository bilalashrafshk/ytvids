# **Title Patterns, the Forge and the Scorecard**

*How the engine produces titles that match what actually worked in the reference set, and how it picks the one to recommend. Built from `references/idea_bank/top_outliers/` (ratio = views ÷ subscribers) and `references/competitors/` (views).*

**Scope:** every channel. A channel profile that defines its own title format (Raahim: "What if…" or a plain absurd statement) replaces the **patterns** in section 2, but not the scorecard's familiarity, puzzle and accuracy checks (section 4).

## 2. The five patterns (fill the slots; do not decorate)

| # | Pattern | Formula | Slots you must be able to fill |
| :-: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **P1** | **Why + everyday subject + surprise** *(preferred)* | "Why [Does/Do] [everyday subject the viewer owns or does] [surprising thing]?" or "How Did [X] [do the impossible]?" | the everyday subject; the surprising thing; the answer (kept out of the title) |
| **P2** | **Constraint hypothetical** | "POV: You [have/are/can only] [constraint]" | the constraint; the twist |
| **P3** | **Comparison with a promise** | "[A] Vs [B] - The Real [Math / Cost / Answer]" | two things the viewer chooses between; what "real" refers to |
| **P4** | **Brand + claim + number** | "[Brand]: [Claim]?" or "[Brand]'s [Event]: [Number] [Thing] Got So Wrong" | a brand a stranger knows; a true, sourced number |
| **P5** | **Stakes ultimatum** | "If You Don't Understand [X], You Don't Understand [bigger thing]" | X; the bigger thing it truly unlocks |

**Rules for filling P1 (the workhorse, used for mechanism stories):**
1. The subject is something a stranger owns or does today: their TV, their phone, their rent, their commute, a coffee. Never an industry term or a group they can't picture ("small TV makers", "freight forwarders").
2. The surprise is a fact from the research, stated as a puzzle: a fee, a reversal, a rule nobody expects.
3. The title asks; the video answers. Never put the answer in the title.
4. Put the search keyword or the brand in the title when it is natural ("…to Use HDMI"), not as a label prefix.
5. Prefer "you" or "your" to "every". "Your TV" is a picture; "every TV on earth" is an overclaim.

## 3. Generation procedure (Phase 1 uses this, in order)

1. **Pass 1, wide and blind (unchanged).** 10 to 15 raw titles, one line each, from the thesis, the premise and the zero-context viewer only. No patterns in view.
2. **Pass 2, the Forge.** Extract four slots from the research brief: (a) the everyday thing the viewer owns or does, (b) the surprising fact about it, (c) the withheld answer, (d) one true number. Then write **at least four P1 candidates** and **one candidate each** for any other pattern that fits. A pattern that cannot fill its slots is skipped, never faked.
3. **Pass 3, the check (fresh pass).** Apply the existing title rules (familiar anchor in the first four words, answer withheld, no overclaims) and the **scorecard** below to every candidate from Passes 1 and 2.
4. **Deliver 4 to 5** covering different angles, as before, but **the recommendation is the highest scorecard total**, never the candidate that felt closest to the working title.

---

## 4. Scorecard (score every candidate, show the scores)

| Test | Points |
| :--- | :-: |
| **Familiar subject:** a zero-context viewer owns or does it (own thing 3, thing they know of 2, a group or industry term 0) | 0–3 |
| **Puzzle:** the title raises a question the video answers, and the answer is not in the title | 0–3 |
| **True:** the verb, the quantifier ("every", "always") and any number match the research and the audit. Any overclaim is a **fail and the title is cut** | 0–2, or cut |
| **Findable:** the keyword or brand appears naturally in the title | 0–1 |
| **Pattern bonus:** P1 or P2 | +1 |
| **Total** | of 10 |

- Recommend only a candidate with **7 or more** and no fail. If none reaches 7, run the Forge again with a different (a) or (b) slot.
- A recommended title that is not P1 or P2 needs one line saying why.

---

## 5. Anti-patterns (each costs the title its recommendation)

- "How [X] Works / Makes Money / Actually Works", "[X] Explained", "The Economics of [X]" as the whole title.
- "The Hidden [X]", "The Secret [X]", "The Truth About [X]" with nothing else.
- Overclaims: "monopoly", "tax", "every … on earth", "always", "never", a single price for a tiered fee.
- The answer given away ("lost, paid, and…", "Here's who collects").
- Specialist-only subject in the first four words.
- A label prefix that adds nothing ("HDMI:" only if it is the natural search term and the rest is already a P1).

**Accuracy on the verb:** "every time you [action]" must match when the money or the effect really happens. If it does not, the title is a hook, not a fact, and the first line of the description must say so.

---

## 6. What to report with the titles

Per title: pattern, closest reference title from section 1, scorecard total, and one line on accuracy. End with: *"This is a pattern check against a small reference set; outlier ratios depend on the channel and on topic demand, so it is not proof of performance. Demand data: [supplied / not supplied]."*
